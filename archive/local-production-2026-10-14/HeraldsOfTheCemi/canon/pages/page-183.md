                         CHAPTER NINETEEN


                       Founders’ Night
            T R AC K   “Dancing in the Street” — Martha and the Vandellas
                          ECHO    the Armory drill hall, 1926




The man at the loading dock wanted to know whether we were
equipment or talent.
    “Both,” said Manny.
    “It doesn’t say both on the list.”
    “It says contributor,” I said, holding up the pass. “And percussion‐
ist.”
    He studied the pass, then the case on the trolley, where I had written
FRAGILE in marker and, because I trust nobody, drawn an arrow
pointing at the stone.
    “What’s that?”
    “A family heirloom.”
    “Is it going to go off?”
    “Not on purpose.”
    He lifted the lid with two fingers, took in the cloth and the foam and
the dark curve underneath, and set it down again as gently as if it
might be somebody’s mother. “Don’t let them put it in the coat check,”
he said, and waved us through.
    By 6:14 we were inside, on schedule for load-in and nearly three
hours ahead of whatever they had prepared for my nervous system.
    The drill hall spread out in front of us.
