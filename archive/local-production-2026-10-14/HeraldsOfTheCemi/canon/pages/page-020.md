                       HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



   My mother looked at me over the cup, and I watched her decide not
to answer. I know that look. It’s the one she gave me at nine when I
asked where Papá went on the nights he wasn’t home, and the one
from the fever. Both times she’d poured more coffee and changed the
subject.
   “Eat lunch,” she said. “A real one. Not coffee.”
   “Promise.”
   “You said that yesterday.”
   “I’ll mean it harder today.”
   She made the sound she makes, the mm with a whole essay inside it,
and turned back to the stove. The coin was in my pocket. I didn’t re‐
member putting it there. It was warm against my hip, and under the ra‐
dio and the salami and my mother’s back, something in me that had
been quiet for twenty-five years cleared its throat.
   I filed it. I took the Ballroom, the dancers, the man in the window,
my grandmother asking a question she already knew the answer to,
and I dropped all of it in the folder with the other forty-seven things,
and I closed the folder, and I went to work.



The empty building across the street had a new banner.
   It had been wrapped in blue netting since fall. Now the netting wore
a vinyl sheet four stories tall: a photo of smiling people in front of a
mural, every skin tone in the Pantone book, and over them in a friendly
rounded font, A CALMER BLOCK STARTS HERE. Under that, a
logo. A camera iris, the blades overlapping in a curve I recognized and
didn’t want to. Aperture Civic. Community Spaces, Coming Soon.
   Two guys in hi-vis were bolting the last corner to the scaffold. The
one holding the ladder had headphones around his neck.
   “Morning,” he said.
   “Morning. You guys work late?”
   “Nights? Nah.” He grinned. “Union. We clock out at three-thirty,
daylight only.”
