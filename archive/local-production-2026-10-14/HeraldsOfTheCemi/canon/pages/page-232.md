                         HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



   “Every one?”
   “Every one. In the front. With a folding chair.”
   “Okay,” I said.
   “Okay,” said Mamá, and held my left hand in both of hers until the
nurse came to take my blood pressure, and then a little after.
   Manny came at noon with his ribs taped and a pillowcase under his
arm. Inside it, the conga, rim cracked end to end, wrapped like a pa‐
tient.
   “Security said no drums,” he said.
   “And?”
   “I said he’s a patient.” He lowered himself into the chair on my left.
“They gave me a visitor sticker for him.”
   The sticker was on the pillowcase. It said CONGA, with a smiley
face.



Sofía came in the afternoon with a laptop.
    She sat on my right out of habit. I didn’t answer. She moved without
comment, took the left, and kept it for the rest of her life.
    “Three things,” she said. “One, the story is out and it’s holding. Two,
don’t get excited, because I don’t do excited. I do verified.” She turned
the screen.
    “Public Schumann monitors,” she said. “There’s a station in Tomsk.
There’s one in Italy. There’s a retired man in Nova Scotia who has
logged this every day for eleven years and posts on a forum like it’s his
marriage.” She tapped. “Last night, at 10:07 Eastern, the line that sits at
about seven point eight three hertz sharpened. Narrowed. Went clean.
It held for eleven minutes. All three stations, the same second.”
    “Your loop pulses at six and a half,” said Sofía. “Maybe it’s been
leaning on that line for years. Maybe I haven’t slept. I can’t tell you why.
I can tell you when. And when is the eleven minutes.”
    “Three. Pri started an inbox for the hub. A form. She posted it
nowhere. It went around by itself.” Sofía held up a stack of printouts.
“Two hundred and twelve messages. Thirty-one countries.”
