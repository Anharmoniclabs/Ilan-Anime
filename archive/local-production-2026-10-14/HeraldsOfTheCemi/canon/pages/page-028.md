                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



   He didn’t believe me. I watched him not believe me. But he also
knew that pushing got him nothing, and he took his hand off my
shoulder slowly, like he wasn’t sure he should, and went back to his
stool and watched me out of the side of his eye for the rest of the hour.
   I finished the latte. I pulled twenty cortados. I did the thing I’m
good at, which is functioning while something is wrong. And some‐
where around cortado fourteen, with the 4 coming through again and
the steamer on a clean A, I got to what had been bothering me about
the email since 10:40.
   It said building-listening.
   Nobody had ever called it that. Not me, not the arts space, not
Manny. I’d shown the arts space a tame version, the one that makes
pretty colors out of laundry-day noise, and I’d called it a sound-to-light
installation, because that’s what you call a thing when you want to get
paid. The one that listens to the building through a steam pipe, that I
had run raw at 3:07 that morning, lived on one laptop, in one closet, on
the fourth floor.
   Somebody had been watching my work, all right. They were just
more specific about it than I’d have liked.
   I took the blank card off the shelf under the register and wrote one
line on it: Ballroom, Saturday nights, mambo bands. Ask Mamá. I
pinned it to the empty corner of Jerome with a yellow pushpin. It was
the only thing I’d written all day that I was sure was true.
   Then I went back to the steamer.
   The 4 came through. The whole room shook, and everyone kept
talking.
