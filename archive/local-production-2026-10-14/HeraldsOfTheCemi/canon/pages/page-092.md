                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



  “We won.”
  “Tavito hace trampa,” said my grandmother, and went inside.
  Tavito cheats.



I didn’t sleep. I lay on my mother’s narrow bed with the window open
to the street, the colmado across the way, a domino game going, a dog
arguing with the dark. The air smelled like rain that hadn’t committed.
I was tired in my bones and happy in a way I hadn’t been in months,
and I’d just about let myself believe it was only that.
    Then I heard it.
    So faint I thought it was the bed. A merengue from the speaker over
the colmado door, tinny and warm. And under it, in the same place in
every bar, a pressed place the width of a hand.
    I got up. I went to the window. Over the colmado door, on a bracket,
was a small white speaker with a decal on it, a camera iris, the blades
overlapping.
    I’d flown two thousand miles.
    The floorboards creaked. My grandmother stood in the doorway in
a flannel robe, her hair down, a rosary in her fist. She wasn’t looking at
me. She was looking at the window, and she was listening the way I’d
learned to listen, with her whole face.
    “Eso llegó en enero,” said Abuela. That arrived in January. “Igual
que allá.”
    Just like back there.
    She didn’t tell me how she knew. She came to the window and stood
beside me and the two of us listened to a speaker over a colmado in the
old city of Santo Domingo, with the domino players slapping tiles un‐
der it, and the dog finally giving up on the dark.
    Her lips were moving. I leaned in. She was counting, silently, with
her eyes on the speaker.
    Seis. Siete. Ocho.
