                           HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



I watched the three dots appear and I caught myself smiling at the
screen.
   Yari is a night-shift nurse at Lincoln, and my ex, though ex is a word
we only use in public. We were together-together a year and a half.
Then we weren’t, and then we were the other thing, the thing where
you’re not together and she’s still the first person you tell things to.
Everybody says that’s a mess. For two years it’s been the steadiest thing
I’ve got. Nights are the only reason she was ever awake at that hour,
and I’d be lying if I said that wasn’t half the reason I texted at 4:38 and
not at a normal time like a normal person.

   Yari, 4:39 a.m.: i’m on break. why are YOU up. and don’t say working
   Me: i’m not working
   Yari: 🤨
   Me: i’m watching a video about adhd

The pause was long. Long enough that I started drafting the walk-back,
the lol nvm it’s nothing. I’ve been leaving rooms before I can be left since
I was about nine. I made myself not send it. I made myself sit in the
pause.
    Then my phone rang.
    She doesn’t text can I call. She calls, which is exactly Yari, and I
picked up on the first ring.
    “Babe. Okay.” The voice she has at four a.m. on a hospital break,
tired and warm and without any patience for nonsense. Behind her, a
vending machine hummed, and under it a monitor was beeping in a
hallway. “What was the video?”
    “How’d you know it was a video?”
    “Because it’s four in the morning and you’re not researching ADHD,
you’re spiraling about yourself and calling it research, which, by the
way, is on the list.” Something rattled, a bag of something. “Which one
got you?”
    So I told her. The Honda. The fires. I call it infrastructure. The trains. I
told her about the eight-minute sprints, which I’d never told anybody,
and she was quiet, and then she said:
    “Oh, babe. The trains.”
