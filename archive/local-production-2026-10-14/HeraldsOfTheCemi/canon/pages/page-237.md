                                MORNING



   On the fourth floor, a Pyrex is how people say the things they won’t
say in the hallway. I’ll tell you what that one said.
   Yes.



Yari and I had dinner the next Friday at a place with real tablecloths. I
got there at 6:52, which had happened four times in my life. She sat
down on my left without being asked, and I noticed, and she noticed
me noticing.
   “Slowly,” she said.
   “Slowly.”
   “Rule one. You tell me you’re late before I’ve ordered.”
   “Done.”
   “Rule two. If I need to leave, I leave, and you don’t follow me to the
door.”
   “Done. What’s three?”
   “Three,” she said, to the menu. “You tell me one thing you want, out
loud, tonight.” I looked at the tablecloth. “To sit on your left,” I said.
“Not because of the ear.” She smiled at the menu so I wouldn’t see how
much. “Order something with a name you can’t pronounce,” she said.
“I want to watch you do it.”



The hub got the Armory for one night.
   It took Sofía eleven weeks, four permits, two meetings of a com‐
munity board that had finally learned the word no, and a single spread‐
sheet tab labeled PLEASE. The date, because there is no other date, was
June thirteenth.
   We called it Remembering Night.
