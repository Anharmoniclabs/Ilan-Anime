                         CHAPTER SEVEN


                     A Name For It
                   T R AC K   “Mack the Knife” — Bobby Darin
                       ECHO     the bank on Webster, 1962




Rafa met me at the park gate at 5:30 Wednesday in a windbreaker the
color of a traffic cone and shoes that looked like they’d walked to Al‐
bany and back.
   “Don’t take the train,” he said. “I’ve been riding the 4 since 1979 and
I have feelings about it. We walk.”
   “It’s two miles.”
   “That’s why I said shoes.”
   He walked slow and steady with his eyes half closed, as if tasting
the street. People parted around him on the sidewalk without noticing
they’d done it. It’s a talent. Somewhere on Jerome he said, without
opening his eyes, “Tell me what you hear.”
   “Traffic. The El. A guy selling tamales who’s lying about the ta‐
males.”
   “Under that.”
   I listened. It took a block. Then it was there, faint, from the open
door of a laundromat: the pressed place the width of a thumb, in the
same spot every eight seconds, under a song about somebody’s heart.
   “That,” Rafa said. He nodded slowly. “Yenni gets seven and a half.
She has better ears or worse moods.”
   So he heard it too. I stood there with my mouth shut and my face
doing whatever it wanted.
