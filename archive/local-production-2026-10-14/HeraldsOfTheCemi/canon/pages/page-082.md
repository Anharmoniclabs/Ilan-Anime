                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ




The footsteps arrived before the man did. I felt them in the banister
first, steady, unhurried, a gentleman’s tempo. By the time he turned the
landing below me, I was on my feet.
    He was tall, gray, courteous, in a good coat, carrying a basket
wrapped in clear plastic with a small card tucked in the ribbon. In the
breast pocket of the coat, a black notebook, its elastic band loose.
    “Neighbor,” said Adrian Vale.
    He stopped two steps below me, so he wouldn’t be looming. I no‐
ticed that. He had the soft voice of a man who has been told his whole
life that he’s kind. It sat low in his chest, and I realized, with a small
bright horror, that it sat exactly where a carrier wave would. About a
hundred and ten.
    “Juan Carlos. I hoped we’d meet.” He smiled. “I’m the one who
wrote you.”
    “I got it.”
    “Yes.” He folded his hands over the basket. “I understand you de‐
clined. A man should be careful whom he trusts. I’d only ask you to
consider that the offer stands.” He waited.
    I’ll tell you what the waiting was like. He spoke in short paragraphs,
and between them he stopped and let the silence do the talking. I star‐
ted counting without meaning to. One, two. By five I could feel it com‐
ing. By eight, the air around us went flat. The pressed place, the width
of a hand. There was no speaker on that landing. It was coming from
the pause.
    “Until the end of April,” Vale said, when the eight seconds were
done. “After that I’m afraid the Founders’ Night program closes to new
work. It would be a pity. Your building-listening work is the most ori‐
ginal thing I’ve seen in twenty years. I include my own.”
    I had my hand on the banister. I didn’t notice that I was tapping it
until I saw him look at it.
