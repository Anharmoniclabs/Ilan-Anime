   In the corridor outside the auditorium, between the water fountain
and the trophy case, under a bulletin board with a flyer for a lost cat, a
hand touched my elbow.
   Vale had waited for the crowd to thin. He stood with the slim port‐
folio under one arm, courteous, unhurried, in the good coat. He did not
look angry. He looked, if anything, pleased. The way a teacher looks at
a student who has finally done the reading.
   “That was very well done,” he said.
   I waited.
   “I want to be clear that I appreciate it. Transparency is a gift.” A
pause. One. Two. “I’m glad you came. I’d like to offer you something.”
   He took a page out of the portfolio and held it out.
   It was the same gray font. The same header. Subject: J. C. Cabral. But
the second line read: ADDENDUM — SUPPLEMENTARY RE‐
SPONSES.
   I took it. I knew I shouldn’t. I read it standing in a school hallway
with a trophy case at my elbow.
   Likely to read assessment aloud at public hearing: 84%.
   Likely to conceal sources: 91%.
   Likely to attempt acoustic retaliation toward staff, week 3: 61%.
   Likely to reestablish contact with subject M. (pilot cohort, level 3): 9%.
   And under that, in a smaller line, in the same neat hand:
   Incident logged. 3rd-floor listening post, glass damage. Documentation re‐
tained.
