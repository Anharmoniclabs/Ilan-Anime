# Manuscript PDF page 99

                                    THE LEDGER



    At the nineteenth page she put a finger on the corner of the bag and
left it there.
    “Ese año se enfermó,” she said. That year she got sick. “Lo trajo
igual.”
    She brought it anyway.
    I took the picture with her finger still in the frame. She didn’t move
it, and I didn’t ask her to.
    Twenty-eight pages, front and back where there was writing on the
back. My arm was shaking a little by the end, and the phone wasn’t
heavy. I sent them to Sofía in one long stack and watched the bar crawl
across the bottom of the screen on my grandmother’s internet, which
she calls el wifi and pays for with the same suspicion she brings to in‐
surance.
    The texts started before the upload finished.

   Sofía: what am i looking at
   Me: my great-grandmother cleaned their offices. she took one page a year for 28
   years. look at the mark at the top
   Sofía: …
   Sofía: jc that’s the iris
   Sofía: who has handled these. what are they stored in. tell me they are not in a
   shoebox
   Me: freezer bags
   Sofía: ok that’s honestly not bad
   Sofía: i need higher resolution and then i need to lie down
   Sofía: also tell your grandmother she’s a better archivist than the city of new york

While she typed, I did the other thing.
    The stone was wrapped again, and the pressed place had crept all
the way back into the merengue across the street. I went to the window.
I opened a spectrum app I’d paid nothing for and had paid for anyway
in casino ads, held the phone against the slats of the shutter, and let it
listen for two minutes.
    It drew the street as a waterfall of color scrolling up the screen. The
moped. The vendor. The merengue, bright and busy across the middle.
And low on the scale, a single thin line that never moved: 110 hertz,
