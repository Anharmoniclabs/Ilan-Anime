                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



    The child hit the drum too hard.
    She startled herself. She looked up.
    Nobody took the drum away.
    The future thinned out like breath on a window, and I let it go.
    I was in the present again, on my left, with Manny’s drum between
his knees, and a boy in a Spider-Man shirt had stopped in front of it
with a juice box. His mother, behind him, was already saying perdón,
perdón, tugging at his sleeve.
    He pointed.
    “Can I?”
    Manny looked at me. The mother looked at me. Behind them, across
forty feet of butcher paper, my grandmother and my mother and my
cousin and a stone that was whole. Somewhere under the steel roof, a
card with a very old name on it.
    “Déjenlo tocar,” I said.
    Let him play.
    The boy put down the juice box. He lifted one small hand over the
head of the drum. He didn’t count. He listened, for a moment, to the
room around him, to everything it was holding, and found the place in
it that was a breath wide, the place it had been leaving open like a chair
at a table.
    And he put the first beat in it.
