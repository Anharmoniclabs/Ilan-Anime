                            CHAPTER TWO


             The Coin Runs Warm
                    T R AC K   “Oye Cómo Va” — Tito Puente
                     ECHO      Discos Quisqueya, August 1973




Mamá was up before the power was. She told me so herself.
    “La luz se fue a las tres y volvió a las cuatro,” she said, turning the
salami with a fork. The radio was low, a merengue station, the kind of
song I could name in two bars with my eyes shut. “Con Ed says trans‐
former. The super says Con Ed. Doña Fela on five says it was the Lord.”
    “What do you say?”
    “I say the keyboard stopped at three-oh-seven.” She didn’t turn
around. “I look at the clock when you don’t sleep. Siéntate.”
    I sat. The chair rocked on its short leg, which had been fixed with a
folded Con Ed bill since 2011. The plate she set down was the full pro‐
duction: mangú, fried salami, queso frito, the tres golpes, the three hits,
which she makes on days she suspects the world is about to hit me
first.
    “Gracias, Má.”
    “Mm.” She leaned on the counter with her coffee and did the inspec‐
tion, the slow one that misses nothing. “Tu abuela llamó anoche. Antes
de que se fuera la luz.”
    I kept chewing. “She good?”
    “She’s old, she’s stubborn, she’s good.” A pause she had clearly re‐
hearsed. “She asked if it already started.”
    The mangú stopped being food. “If what started?”
