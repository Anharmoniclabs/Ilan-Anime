                      C H A P T E R T W E N T Y- O N E


                      Pocket vs Click
          T R AC K   “The Girl from Ipanema” — Stan Getz and João Gilberto
                        ECHO    the Armory boxing ring, 1969




I’m going to tell this part the way it happened, which is all at once.
   The reception speakers were playing bossa nova. I want that under‐
stood. Somebody at Aperture had decided that a celebration of calm
should have an elevator soundtrack, and so, as eleven hundred phones
began to breathe rain and a hundred and ten hertz crawled up through
the carpet, Stan Getz was blowing his sweet, lazy saxophone out of six
ceiling speakers, and the girl from Ipanema walked, tall and tan and
young and lovely, through the one fight I will ever be in.
   The first attendant reached for the trolley.
   He was a young guy in a gray polo, with an earbud and a lanyard
and the careful face of a person who’d been told this was a customer-
service situation. He put his hand on the handle. I turned the trolley a
quarter turn. His hand closed on air.
   I didn’t think. I’d spent weeks learning to hold a room, and what I’d
actually learned was where its gaps are.
   The staff were moving to a click. I could feel it now, through the car‐
pet and the soles of my shoes, a metronome at a hundred and twenty
beats a minute, sunk in every ear on the wall: left, right, left, right. A
grid. A grid is a thing you can stand on, and a grid is a thing you can
fall through.
