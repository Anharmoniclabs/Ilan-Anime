                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



   “After’s a start.” He took the thumb drive out of my hand, gently, the
way you take scissors from a child. “Gimme. I’ll give it to Sofía. You’re
not holding this one.”
   I let him.
   At home, in the kitchen, Mamá was standing at the shelf with the
glasses, with her back to me. She didn’t turn around. She was counting.
   “Siete,” she said. Seven.
   “Má—”
   “The Pathmark closed. You can’t get that set anymore.” Her voice
was level. “I’m not asking.”
   I stood in the doorway with a cut on my palm I hadn’t noticed from
a window I’d never touched, and a drive I’d given away, and a night I
couldn’t unmake.
   “I broke it,” I said. “And I hurt someone. A kid. A contractor. I’m go‐
ing to fix the kid first.”
   She turned around. She looked at my face, and I saw her decide that
this was the answer she’d been waiting for, and that it was sufficient.
   “Siéntate,” said my mother. “Eat. Then we buy a glass.”
