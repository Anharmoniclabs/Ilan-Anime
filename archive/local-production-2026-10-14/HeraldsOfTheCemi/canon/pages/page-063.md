   “Is something wrong with me?”
   The circle went quiet. Even the kids.
   Rafa took a long time. He’s a man who takes his time. Then he said,
“No.” Simple. Flat. “Nothing wrong. A lot loud. And in this country,
hijo, loud and wrong get filed in the same drawer.”
   He picked up his drum and tucked it under his arm.
   “Come back Wednesday. Bring the thing in your pocket you keep
touching.”
   “What—”
   “Nine times since you sat down.” He didn’t smile. “I counted. I’m
old, I don’t have a lot else to do.” He started across the clearing. “Wear
shoes you can walk in. I want to show you your block.”



I sat on the stone ring a long time after the circle broke up.
    Manny dropped onto the stone beside me, which made it sag, and
said nothing for about a minute, which for Manny is a hunger strike.
    “I told Rafa about you a year ago,” he said finally. “I hope that’s
okay.”
    “A year ago?”
    “You’d have bailed. You bail on everything you can’t be good at the
first time.” He looked at his hands. “I’m not smart, JC. I know I’m not
smart. But I know how to shut up for a year.”
    I didn’t have anything for that, so I bumped his shoulder with mine,
which is the closest we get.
    “You don’t have to explain it to me,” he said. “Whatever it is. Just
don’t do it by yourself.”
    “You neither.”
    “I’m never by myself,” said Manny. “I’m too loud.”
    Then I took out my phone and did something I’d been putting off
for nine days.
