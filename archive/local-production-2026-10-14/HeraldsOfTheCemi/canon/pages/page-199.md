Left foot. Right foot. A line of gray polos along the wall, advancing by
inches.
   On the sound desk, Danny’s hand went to his own ear. He pulled
the earbud half out. He looked at me over four hundred heads and
mouthed a word.
   Click.
   The click-track. They’d been wearing it all night. They’d been work‐
ing to a beat.
   I looked at the clock on the wall. 9:36.
   Across the hall, Rafa lifted one hand, palm out. Stop.
   And then, in the same hand, he pointed to the north door.
   Out.
   Behind the stage, the door to the green room opened, and two at‐
tendants stepped out, in step, with a supervisor between them. The
loading curtain at the north end of the hall swung across the passage,
drawn by a man in a gray polo with a radio at his lip.
   The shortest route to our people outside disappeared.
   “Please remain where you are, Mr. Cabral,” said Vale, into the mi‐
crophone, with enormous courtesy.
   9:38.
   The attendants started toward us.
   I tightened the strap across La Madre’s case, just enough to hold her
on the trolley. Manny shifted his drum toward the lip of the little stage
and planted his feet. In my pocket, the coin had gone from warm to
something I could feel through the denim.
   On the screen, the number had stopped climbing. A new line ap‐
peared beneath it, in the same soft gray.
   MOMENT OF CALM — 9:40 PM.
   Every phone in the hall lit at once.
   A thousand small screens, a thousand leaves. A single bar of rain
began, from eleven hundred pockets, eleven hundred hands, and be‐
neath it, in the same place every eight seconds, I felt it settle across the
room like a hand across a table.
   “You take the left,” said Manny, very softly.
   “Where’s the door?”
