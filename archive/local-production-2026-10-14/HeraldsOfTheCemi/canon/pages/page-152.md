   I hadn’t slept right since the window. I kept seeing a boy fold over
with his hands on his head. Danny called me on a Tuesday at 4:01 p.m.,
exactly, to say his ear was healing and the doctor said it would be fine,
probably, which I wrote on my arm in marker so I could read it back to
myself whenever I needed to.
   Then he said he was coming to the café, and he wanted to bring
someone.



The someone was a woman in a good jacket and sensible shoes, stand‐
ing very straight just inside the door as if the floor might give.
   “This is Vanessa,” said Danny. “She’s the chairs lady.”
   “I book the chairs,” said Vanessa. “And the rooms, and the facilitat‐
ors, and the water.” She looked at the espresso machine with the faint
horror of a woman in unfamiliar territory. “I work in community pro‐
gramming at Aperture.”
   Manny, behind the counter, dropped a spoon.
   Sofía didn’t move. Sofía put down her pen, very precisely, and fol‐
ded her hands, which is what she does instead of screaming.
   “Sit down,” she said, “and tell me why you’re here before I decide to
be rude.”
   Vanessa sat. She put her phone on the table and a manila folder be‐
side it. I noticed she hadn’t taken her badge off. I think she wanted us
to know she wasn’t hiding it.
   “I was asked to book follow-up visits,” she said. “Housing referrals,
community-support enrollment, for households that are expected to be
displaced.”
   “Expected,” said Sofía.
   “Yes.”
   “By whom?”
   “By the report.” She opened the folder.
