                           POCKET VS CLICK



   The event manager, who’d been standing near the curtain, took one
step backward.
   “I’m also a poet,” said Mr. Rodríguez, to nobody, and wrote down a
time.
   On the platform, Mikey lifted his head.
   For a heartbeat the hum in the ring went ragged. Vale, at the lectern,
turned his head and said, very softly, so softly the microphone barely
caught it: “Mikey.”
   Mikey’s head went down.
   The hum steadied.
   I saw Doña Luz stand up in the front row.
   She didn’t say a word. She stood there in her church dress with her
purse in both hands, and Vale’s head turned, and for the first time that
night, I saw his face do something it hadn’t planned.
   “Sit down, señora,” said an usher.
   She didn’t.



I don’t know how long we were there. I know that in the middle of it,
with Stan Getz still going and Mikey’s name in my mouth and the su‐
pervisor circling and the staff in a knot, I felt the floor tilt and looked
toward the north curtain and saw someone I hadn’t been looking for.
   Danny was running.
   He’d come off the sound desk at a flat sprint with his lanyard flying
and his bandaged ear, straight down the side of the hall, in the shad‐
ows by the wall. He didn’t look at me. He hit the loading curtain at full
speed and shoved through it and was gone.
   I heard it from where I stood. A beep. A badge on a reader.
   The curtain jumped.
   Then a sound, from the other side of the north wall, from beyond
the brick, thin and flat and unfinished, one note from a throat made of
bamboo.
   A horn.
