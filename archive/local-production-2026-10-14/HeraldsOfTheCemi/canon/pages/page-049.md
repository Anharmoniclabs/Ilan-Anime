                        ECHO     the back bedroom, 1972




At 4:11 on Saturday morning I was on top of my covers with a hot
laptop, doing what any reasonable person does after a glyph rises out
of a café wall and a stranger nods at him from a doorway. I was search‐
ing why does noise help me think.
   The first four results were about productivity and a coffee shop in
Portland. The fifth was a study from 2007. In plain English: they gave
kids a memory task with and without white noise playing, and the
kids with ADHD did better with the noise on, and the kids without it
did worse. The researchers said a brain running low on its own internal
noise needs some from outside, the way a plant leans toward a win‐
dow.
   I closed the tab like it was a stove.
   I don’t have ADHD. I want that on the record, because I made the
case to myself right then, out loud, to an empty room, and I thought it
was airtight. ADHD is a kid who can’t sit still. ADHD is Hector Beltrán,
who ate glue in fifth grade and now sells timeshares. I’m not hyper. I’m
early to gigs. I finish things.
   Do I?
   I finish things on trains. I’d never thought about that either, except
that I had. I’d just never said it out loud.
