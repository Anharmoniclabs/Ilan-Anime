                           CHAPTER FOURTEEN


                              Paper Trail
                T R AC K   “El Costo de la Vida” — Juan Luis Guerra y 440
                                ECHO     our kitchen, 2007




Sofía had converted her mother’s dining room into something
between a newsroom and a crime scene.
   Her mother, Doña Aurora, had accepted the string on three condi‐
tions: that nothing touched the saint in the corner, that nobody put a
pushpin in the good tablecloth, and that everyone who worked there
was fed. She’d been hovering at the doorway with a tray of coffee for
twenty minutes, not asking questions, which in a Dominican mother is
a form of surveillance.
   The wall was a time line. Sofía had drawn it on butcher paper, like
Mr. Rodríguez’s map, six columns, one name at the top of each, and a
red string running through them like a vein.

   1523–1890s: Abertura y Cía., Factores. Sugar, shipping, a ledger. The Santo
   Domingo office kept the old name until 1965.
   1890s–1924: Abertura & Co., Shipping and Insurance. Insurance. “They started
   insuring the ships,” Sofía said. “Then they started insuring the cargo. Then they
   started insuring the things the cargo was.”
   1924–1968: Meridian Sound & Radio. Recording studios, a record label, a radio-
   parts factory.
   1968–2007: Meridian Acoustics. Sound-masking systems for offices, schools,
   hospitals. And, in a line Sofía had underlined twice, field surveys: Newgrange,
   1968. Giza, 1971. Chavín de Huántar, 1974. Salisbury Plain, 1977. Teotihuacan,
   1979.
   2007–2024: Quiet Hours Foundation.
