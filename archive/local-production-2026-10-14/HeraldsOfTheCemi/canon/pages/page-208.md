                         HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



    It blew once. It stopped, as if whoever held it had lowered it to
listen.
    Then a second one answered.
    “Manny,” I said.
    “I hear it.” He was straightening up, slowly, one hand on his side.
His face was gray. He was smiling. “That’s Tete.”
    The curtain at the north end of the hall shuddered, and two ushers
dragged at it, and it didn’t hold. A strip of dark appeared at the bot‐
tom. Night air came through, smelling of exhaust and rain and the
Bronx.
    And beyond it, framed in the gap of two steel doors, with a bamboo
horn at his lips and a lantern at his feet, stood a boy in a red shirt, nine‐
teen years old, blowing a single note.
    Behind him, the drums began.
