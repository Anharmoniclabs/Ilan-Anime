Abuela had been watching me over the top of her glasses.
  “¿Qué dice la muchacha?”
  “She says it’s the same sound. Here and at home.”
  “Claro que es el mismo.” She took off the glasses and folded them.
“Son los mismos.”
  Of course it’s the same. They’re the same people.
  I scrolled up. “She also says you’re a better archivist than the city of

  “Eso ya lo sé,” said my grandmother.



I helped her put the pages back. It took a while. Every Ziploc went in
the order she’d taken it out, because the order mattered, because the
pages were a year each, and twenty-eight years was a person’s whole
working life.
   Under the Ziplocs, tied with kitchen string, was a bundle of older
letters she hadn’t taken out. Thin airmail paper with red and blue
edges, stamps from Ireland, Egypt, Peru, Mexico, England. Different
hands. A few in a Spanish nobody in my family would have written.
