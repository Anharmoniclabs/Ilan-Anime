                                       PRESSURE



   It was a single page, tidy, with the iris in the corner. OBSERVATION
REPORT: PROPERTY CONDITIONS. A list of four addresses. The café.
Our building, with the Ballroom on the ground floor. Two I didn’t
know. Beside each, a status: Unsafe occupancy — closure recommended.
   Sofía read it twice.
   “The date,” she said.
   “June fourteenth.”
   “And the box below it?”
   Vanessa swallowed. “Observed conditions. June thirteenth.”
   The espresso machine hissed behind us. Nobody moved.
   What I understood, sitting there, was that I’d been wrong about the
shape of it for months. I’d thought Aperture was building up to a cere‐
mony. They were building up to an incident. The ceremony was the
stage. The incident was the paperwork: a night so bad, a room so
frightening, a participant so alarming, that four buildings on one block
would be found unfit by the following morning. Somebody had
already written what they would see.
   “Somebody wrote the weather report,” said Manny, slowly, “before
the storm.”
   Sofía looked at him. It was the first time I’d seen her look at Manny
as though he were the smartest person in the room.
   Vanessa had one more sheet in the folder, folded in half, as if she
hadn’t decided whether to show us. FOUNDERS’ NIGHT — RUN OF
SHOW (INTERNAL). The four movements she’d been told were tech‐
nical, with times.

   I. Gathering. Registered attendance plus app holders, borough-wide.
   II. Counting. Room and devices in sync. Pilot cohort (40) seated as reference.
   III. Calm. 9:40 p.m. Expected post-event quiescence: borough-wide.
   IV. Seating. 10:00 p.m. Yield retained in anchor. Pilot sites to follow.

Sofía laid it beside the observation report and put a finger on each.
“The report is the paperwork. That’s what a judge reads. The count is
the job. And this”—the last line—“is the point. They don’t want the
Bronx calm for a night. They want to keep what comes off it, in some‐
thing they can carry.”
