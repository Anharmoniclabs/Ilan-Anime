                         CHAPTER SEVENTEEN


                                     Diner
                     T R AC K   “Gracias a la Vida” — Mercedes Sosa
                    ECHO     the Olympia Diner, Christmas Eve 1998




The text came at 11:40, the night of the hearing, while I was sitting on
my bed holding a page that said I was sixty-one percent likely to be a
problem.

   Mikey: diner. saturday 7am. the one on the concourse
   Mikey: come hungry
   Mikey: dont bring the girl with the papers

I read it eight times. I wrote back ok, then deleted it, then wrote ok
again, because it was the only word I had that didn’t sound like either
begging or a speech.
    The Olympia has been closing for as long as I’ve been alive, accord‐
ing to the sign in the window, and it has never once closed. It sits on
the Concourse between a pharmacy and a phone store, with a re‐
volving dessert case, a jukebox that takes quarters, and a counterman
named Gus, who looks about ninety and has the posture of a man
who’s been told the world is ending and has decided to finish the shift
first.
    Mikey was already in a booth when I came in.
    He stood up.
    That was new. The Mikey I remembered didn’t get up for anybody.
