                              THE CHORD



    They raised me onto the stretcher and I saw the drill hall from be‐
low: tables askew, torn carpet, plain brick above the temporary walls. A
custodian had started sweeping broken glass into a corner, where
nobody leaving would step in it.
    It was a big, tired room, and it had come through the night with the
rest of us.
    At the open doors I turned my head to the right out of habit, to hear
the street. Nothing came back. I turned left instead.
    Mikey walked beside me until the medic needed the space.
    “Go home,” I told him.
    He held up his phone. His mother’s name was already lit.
    “I am.”
    They loaded me left side first, so I could watch the doors close. The
siren started on my left. On my right there was nothing, and I couldn’t
tell which of them was louder.
