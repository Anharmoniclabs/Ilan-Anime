Here is the part nobody believes.
   Duane came for me again, and I put my back against the platform
rail, and the rail was steel, and my palm went flat on it.
   A boxing ring. The same hall, 1969. A canvas square under hot
lights, ropes, a bell, two hundred men in shirtsleeves roaring, a fog of
cigar smoke rolling to the roof. In the middle, a welterweight in white
trunks with his guard up, getting hit.
   Not getting hit. I watched. The other man threw a right, and it
landed on the ropes. He threw a left, and it landed on air. The fighter in
white moved a half-beat before every punch, a little early, a little off,
not away, just not there. The other man kept swinging at where he’d
been.
   Every time, an inch to the side. Every time, a half-beat early.
   The bell rang.
   The canvas went. I was against a steel rail with a big man’s hand
coming at my collar, and I slid left a half-beat before it came, and his
fingers closed on nothing, and he went past me into the platform rail
with a clang.
   The hum from the ring stuttered. Forty throats caught on a breath.
   Vale’s voice came out of the ceiling.
   “Juan Carlos.” Warm, patient, from six speakers at once, right on top
of Stan Getz. “Please sit. Your friend needs help.”
   A pause. One, two. Three.
   Both sentences could have been true. He let the silence lean on them.
   I didn’t sit.
