                           ECHO    Abuela’s kitchen, 1961




Abuela’s trunk lived under her bed, behind a row of shoes that
hadn’t been worn since the Balaguer years.
   It was a steamer trunk, canvas and wood with brass corners, and she
lined it herself with newspaper from 1961, which she showed me first,
before anything else, because the newspaper was her proof that the
trunk was real. “Mira la fecha,” she said, tapping a headline about a
dictator who no longer existed. “It has been here longer than your
mother.”
   Inside, the pages weren’t in an archival box. They were in Ziploc
bags, the freezer kind, quart size, because Abuela does not trust any
method of preserving a thing that she can’t see through.
   I put my hand on the brass latch to lift the lid. It was cold.
   The kitchen changed.
