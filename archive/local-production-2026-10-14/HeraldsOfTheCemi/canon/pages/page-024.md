The email came in at 10:40, between a guy who wanted oat milk in a
cortado (a crime) and the freelancer at the corner table who’d been
nursing one drip coffee for three hours (a saint, an icon, leave her
alone).
   My phone buzzed. I told myself I wouldn’t look. I looked.

   From: Partnerships Team, Aperture Civic
   Subject: Juan Carlos — we’ve been watching your work
   We’ve been watching your work. Your building-listening practice is the most
   original signal-to-visual work we’ve seen from an independent artist in two years,
   and we think you’re solving a problem most of the industry doesn’t know it has
   yet. We’d like to put real resources behind it, starting with a commission for
   Founders’ Night, our June celebration at the Kingsbridge Armory: hardware,
   compute, a development budget with no strings on the creative. We know what
   the grind costs. We’d like to take it off you. — Adrian Vale, Director of Community
   Partnerships

I read it twice. The second time was worse.
    Because they were right. That’s how it works. If it had been flattery, I
could have ignored it. Flattery is generic. This was exact. They had
named the one thing I was proud of, the thing nobody had noticed, the
thing I built because I couldn’t not. And I was standing behind a
counter I’d be behind for six more hours, nine days from rent, running
on a diet of cafecito and spite, and somebody was offering to take the
grind off me.

from a stranger can carry me for a week, and one bad one can sink me
