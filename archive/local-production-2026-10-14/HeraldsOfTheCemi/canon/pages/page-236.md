                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ




   Me: yeah

I got to the door before the typing dots finished appearing. I remember
I didn’t brush my teeth. I opened it and he was sitting on the landing
step with his knees up, in a gray hoodie, one shoulder higher than the
other, his phone in both hands like a cup of something hot.
   He looked at me.
   I sat down next to him.
   Neither of us said anything for a while. A 4 went by, far off. The
caged bulb above us buzzed. Somewhere in the building a baby got up
and was talked back to sleep.
   “I’m not asking you for anything,” said Mikey. “I couldn’t sleep. I
wanted to see if the light was on.”
   “It was on.”
   “I know. I saw it under the door.”
   We sat with that.
   He put his ear against the wall between 4C and 4D, the way we used
to. I put my left ear against it. For a while we were two grown men
pressing our heads to plaster in the middle of the night like kids who’d
lost a bet.
   “Can you hear it?” he said.
   “Not on this side.”
   “Me neither.” He almost laughed. “I’ve been trying for a week. The
hum. It’s like when the fridge dies and you still think it’s going.” He
kept his ear on the wall. “It’s just a building, JC.”
   “It’s just a building.”
   His hand came up, slowly, to his left temple. Two fingers.
   Dos.
   He didn’t hold it for eight seconds. He didn’t count. He held it for as
long as he wanted, and lowered it when he was done.
   Across the landing the door of 4C opened a hand’s width. Doña Luz
stood in the crack in a housecoat and a hairnet, saying nothing at all.
She set a glass dish on the mat, steaming, with foil over it, and closed
the door.
   It was our Pyrex. Washed, and filled with arroz con habichuelas.
