                            CHAPTER EIGHT


                                  Mikey
             T R AC K   “Pedro Navaja” — Rubén Blades and Willie Colón
                  ECHO     the fourth-floor landing, two winters ago




I told you at the beginning I’d start with the part I’m not proud of. I
lied a little. 3:07 was the part I was willing to say.
   This is the other part.
   Two winters ago, at 1:14 on a Thursday morning, Mikey texted me
two words. you up. I was up. I was lying in bed with the phone lit on
my chest, and I watched the words arrive, and I put the phone face-
down on the mattress and looked at the ceiling until I fell asleep.
   I never told anyone that. I’m telling you.
   Mikey and I grew up on the same landing, apartments 4C and 4D,
one wall between us, which is why we had a language of knocks before
we could spell. At nine he fell off the fire escape and got a scar through
his left eyebrow that he wore like a signature. At fifteen I found music
and Manny, and he found the guys who were loudest on the corner,
and I didn’t follow him. I’d like to tell you that was wisdom. It was that
my head couldn’t commit even to trouble. We drifted. No fight. Two
boats on two currents. Then he stopped answering my texts, and then
one night, two words came the other way.
   For two years I’d walked past the door of 4C every day. I knew its
wreath, which had been up since Christmas of some previous presid‐
ency. I knew its mat, which said BENDICIONES. I knew the exact
sound my keys made when I was trying not to make it.
