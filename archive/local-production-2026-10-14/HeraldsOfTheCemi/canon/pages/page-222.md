                        HERALDS OF THE CEMÍ



   I reached for the burr. I couldn’t help it. I wanted to smooth it, to
help.
   Pain went through my hand so hard that the hall turned white.
   The bowl wasn’t mine. Her roof wasn’t mine. Her note, burr and all,
was the whole point of her, and I’d put a thumb on it.
   Salma said something sharp in Arabic. I didn’t know the words. I
knew the tone: leave it alone. I laughed, soundlessly, and Yanaisa stared
at my face with alarm.
   The burr went out across the dark with the others, and something
caught it. Niamh’s low drum. Ruth’s gap. They didn’t blend. They
stayed three separate things, and the whole of it was stronger because
there was somewhere for the rough one to hold.
   Back in the hall I sensed the counterfeit baseline casting about for a
single repeating interval to settle into. Each time it found a footing,
some stranger’s answer shifted the floor under it.



“Tap,” said Manny.
    I hadn’t heard him. He’d tapped the rim of his cracked conga twice,
the way we’d rehearsed on the stairs, thirty seconds ago and thirty
seconds before that, and I hadn’t tapped back. He tapped again.
    I tried to lift my hand. The hand was on La Madre and had gone
somewhere without me.
    He waited. Four seconds. He looked at my face, and I watched him
decide, with all the terrible speed of a man who’d never once asked for
the plan to be explained and had memorized it anyway, and he reached
across and yanked the contact cable out of the interface.
    Nothing happened.
    The cable had never been what held me. The Garden went dark on
its screen and the stones didn’t notice. He stared at the dead plug in his
fist, and I saw it dawn on him: the whole plan rested on a cable, and
the cable was a nail in a river.
    “JC,” said Manny. “Where’s the door?”
