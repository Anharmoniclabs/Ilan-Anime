    “What would I say? ‘A man came, I turned on the radio’?” She took
the letter out of my hands, gently, and smoothed it flat. “They offered a

Tía Dolores gave us a loan and never let me forget it.” She almost
smiled. “You were a good boy. You tapped on the table and I said, Ay,
qué niño inquieto, and I thought, this is a boy who needs noise, and I gave
you noise. I didn’t know what I was doing.”
    I stood there with my hand open where the letter had been.
    “Luz said yes,” said my mother. “She had two jobs. She had no
choice. I had a mother in Santo Domingo who told me to say no.” She
looked at me, unblinking. “That doesn’t make me a better mother. I got
a different phone call.”
    I couldn’t speak. Manny had put an arm around her. Mama Tete had
turned her face away, with a hand over her mouth, in the way of a wo‐
man who has been at too many deathbeds to cry in front of strangers.
Sofía had stopped taking notes.
