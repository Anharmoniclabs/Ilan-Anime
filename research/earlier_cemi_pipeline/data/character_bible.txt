Heralds of the Cemí — Character List
Every character in the novel, with a locked look for drawing
Luis M. Minier
Table of Contents

Each entry gives the character’s role and a locked look for model sheets. Details the book does not state are marked (adaptation choice), so you can change them freely; everything else comes from the text. The bracketed tag (for example [JC]) is the name used in the comic scripts’ image prompts. Variants (younger or older versions) are listed under the main entry.
Core
[JC]: Juan Carlos “JC” Cabral
•	Role: Narrator. A 25-year-old Bronx sound and AR artist and café barista who hears what is under a room and can hold or push it.
•	Look: 25-year-old Dominican-American man. Lean to skinny build (“flaco”) at medium height (adaptation choice: 5’9”). Light-brown skin (adaptation choice). Narrow tired face with dark under-eye shadows. Dark almond eyes, the same family eyes as Mamá and Abuela (adaptation choice: the shared feature that marks the line). Short dark curly hair, faded at the sides by Papo and Wilson’s barbershop (adaptation choice). Light stubble (adaptation choice). Default outfit is a faded charcoal hoodie (color: adaptation choice), dark jeans with the warm coin in the right front pocket, worn sneakers, and black over-ear headphones around his neck. His fingers are restless and tap on whatever is nearby. For Founders’ Night he wears his best shirt, a white button-down that Mamá checked for buttons (color: adaptation choice), with dark trousers.
•	Signature props: The coin, Chismosa’s contact-mic puck, a laptop, and an occasional thin nosebleed line.
•	Continuity: From Ch. 23 on, he is deaf in the right ear and turns his left ear toward sound. A small round burn sits on his right palm, coin-sized, from the coin (adaptation choice: placement). From Ch. 15 he has a cut on his palm.
•	Variant [JC-2057]: Old man seen only from behind or in profile with his face hidden. A tiny device sits behind his right ear and he holds a cup. His knee posture marks him.
[MANNY]: Manuel “Manny” (gives the alias “Pérez”, his aunt’s surname)
•	Role: JC’s best friend since age nine. A 25-year-old percussionist whose palm on a drum puts a room in step.
•	Look: 25-year-old Afro-Dominican man, six-foot-three, “built like a refrigerator that learned to dance”. Huge rounded shoulders, a thick chest and enormous hands. Dark-brown skin (adaptation choice). Broad open face with a wide easy grin and expressive eyebrows, and he sticks his tongue out of the mouth corner when tuning. Close-cropped hair with a sharp lineup (adaptation choice). Casual default is an oversized plain tee or Knicks T-shirt, track pants and sneakers (adaptation choice, drawn from the Knicks tee in Ch. 15), and he is usually carrying a hard drum case or two iced coffees. For Founders’ Night he wears a rented black tuxedo built for a normal-sized man: the sleeves stop halfway up his forearms, the bow tie is askew, and the seam splits under one shoulder. From Ch. 24 his ribs are taped.
•	Signature prop: Manny’s conga.
[MIKEY]: Mikey (only ever “Mikey”)
•	Role: JC’s childhood neighbor from 4C. Scored “level three” on the Calm Index and now works as an Aperture “community ambassador” who hums the loop.
•	Look: 25-year-old Latino man with a lean, slightly wiry build (adaptation choice) and olive-tan skin (adaptation choice). A pale scar runs as a thin line through his left eyebrow. His left shoulder dips on every step, so he carries one shoulder visibly higher than the other. His face is very calm and blank, “a screen between channels”, with tired, clear dark eyes. Fresh, neat, company-paid haircut. He wears a clean gray jacket over a dark shirt, often with a ball cap pulled low. In landing scenes (2024 and Ch. 24) he wears a gray hoodie with his knees up and his phone held in both hands. His hands are folded or in his pockets, and he lines up silverware precisely.
•	Signature gesture: Dos: two fingers to the left temple, or two flat knocks.
[MAMA]: Doña Altagracia Cabral, “Mamá”
•	Role: JC’s mother, 53. She left Santo Domingo 31 years ago, turned down Vale in 2007 by turning the radio up, and keeps the papers drawer and the Café Bustelo can.
•	Look: 53-year-old Dominican woman of sturdy medium build (adaptation choice) with medium-brown skin (adaptation choice). She has the family’s dark almond eyes and a level, all-seeing “inspection” stare. Dark hair streaked with gray (gray: adaptation choice), pulled back. At home she wears a house blouse and slacks (adaptation choice) with a dish towel over one shoulder and a rosary wrapped around her fist. Out of the house she wears her church coat (color: adaptation choice, deep plum wool) and carries her purse on her knees and a folding chair from home.
•	Variant [MAMA-2007]: 34 years old, dark hair pulled back with no gray, dish towel on her shoulder, standing in her kitchen.
[ABUELA]: JC’s grandmother (Altagracia’s mother), Santo Domingo
•	Role: 79-year-old keeper of La Madre and the 28-page ledger. “Ellos cuentan. Nosotros recordamos.”
•	Look: 79-year-old Dominican woman, small and slightly stooped. Deep tan-brown weathered skin (adaptation choice). Thinning white hair. The family’s dark almond eyes in a face that misses nothing. She stands in doorways with her arms folded. Daily wear is a cotton housedress with a dish towel over one shoulder. At night she wears a flannel robe with her hair down and a rosary in her fist. “Dressed for the work” means a plain dark dress, hair pulled back hard, sandals and a straw bag over her arm. She wears reading glasses to supervise. For JFK in Ch. 24 she wears her good dress and funeral shoes and carries a bodega plastic bag.
•	Variant [ABUELA-2009]: 62 years old, in a housedress with her hair tied in a scarf, at the river wall.
•	Variant [ABUELA-1961]: A girl of about 14 in a nightgown in a doorway, with the same eyes.
[YANAISA]: Yanaisa, JC’s cousin (“la hija de mi sobrina”, the daughter of Abuela’s niece)
•	Role: Paramedic from Villa Mella and keeper of Yúcahu. She decides when the two stones meet.
•	Look: Dominican woman of about 25, athletic and compact (adaptation choice), with warm brown skin (adaptation choice). Strong jaw. A level, skeptical stare “like a package delivered to the wrong apartment”. Dark hair in a tight low bun (adaptation choice). Default is a navy paramedic uniform with the sleeves pushed up (color: adaptation choice) and a motorcycle helmet under one arm. For the procession and Founders’ Night (Ch. 22–23) she wears a blue skirt she hemmed herself and a plain top, with a woven sling across her chest carrying Yúcahu. In Ch. 24 she wears the woven sling again for travel.
[SOFIA]: Sofía
•	Role: Researcher for the tenant association. She builds the paper case: shells, the Calm Index, the run-of-show and the reporters.
•	Look: Dominican-American woman of about 26, small and quick (adaptation choice), with light-brown skin (adaptation choice). Sharp, narrow-eyed “prosecuting” face. Dark hair in a practical claw-clip twist (adaptation choice). Rectangular glasses (adaptation choice). Default is a cardigan over jeans and a tote (adaptation choice) with an open laptop. Her props are a clipboard marked INCIDENTS and a security-guard clicker (Ch. 4) and a notebook labeled DATA. For Founders’ Night she wears a black dress and carries a handbag holding four phones. Ch. 24 adds a chicken-shaped egg timer.
[YARI]: Yari
•	Role: JC’s ex. A night-shift ER nurse at Lincoln Hospital and the first person he tells things to.
•	Look: Afro-Latina woman of about 27, tall and steady (adaptation choice), with dark-brown skin (adaptation choice). Tired, warm eyes and a no-nonsense mouth. Box braids pulled up in a high bun (adaptation choice). Default is ceil-blue hospital scrubs (color: adaptation choice) with a jacket over them, a first-aid bag on her shoulder and her phone. At the restaurant in Ch. 24 she wears a simple dark dress (adaptation choice).
[YENNI]: Yenni
•	Role: Elder from Rafa’s circle and a daycare worker. She is the woman who nods at JC from the café doorway and plays the shekere.
•	Look: Woman of about 70, “old the way a building is old”, upright and not frail. Medium-brown skin (adaptation choice). Silver hair pulled back hard. A corner-of-the-mouth smile. She wears a cardigan the color of dried tobacco and carries a canvas grocery bag. She sometimes wears an I WAS BRAVE daycare sticker and carries a clipboard. Her prop is a shekere in her lap.
•	Variant [YENNI-1991]: About 35, on a plastic crate playing the shekere, with the same jaw angle.
The Vales
[VALE]: Adrian Vale, Director of Community Partnerships, Aperture Civic
•	Role: Present-day antagonist. Counts the block, writes everyone into his notebook, and uses eight-second pauses.
•	Look: Tall, slender man in his mid-50s with pale skin (adaptation choice). Long courteous face with a soft, patient half-smile and calm gray eyes (adaptation choice). Neat silver-gray hair. Overall he reads as “tall, gray, courteous”. He wears a good charcoal-gray overcoat over a dark suit, with the coat sometimes carried over his arm, and a small black notebook with a loose elastic band in his breast pocket. His posture is unhurried and he stops two steps away so as not to loom.
•	Variant [VALE-2007]: Same man about 19 years younger with dark hair, in the gray coat with his hat in hand.
[ANTHONY-VALE]: Dr. Anthony Vale, founder of Meridian Acoustics (1968)
•	Role: The watcher in the 1962 bank and at the 1973 record-shop counter.
•	Look: Thin man in his 40s (age: adaptation choice) with pale skin (adaptation choice). Narrow, still face. Slicked dark hair (adaptation choice). He wears a brown suit with a small notebook in the breast pocket. His pen moves “quick and neat, a mark for each”. The way he holds his head matches Adrian’s: tilted and listening.
[ALAN-VALE]: Alan Vale, Community Outreach (1991)

He wears a short-sleeved white dress shirt and a plain tie and carries a small notebook and pencil. In 1991 he leans against a tree. In 1998 he sits in the corner booth, puzzled.
Bronx
[RAFA]: Rafa
•	Role: Leader of the Sunday drum circle (the areíto) in his 60s. He teaches JC to hear, hold, and not push.
•	Look: Afro-Latino man well past sixty, lean and unhurried (build: adaptation choice), with deep-brown skin (adaptation choice). His face is calm, with eyes half-closed “tasting the street”. Gray locs pulled back. His hands look carved from the same wood as his drum. For walks he wears a traffic-cone-orange windbreaker and shoes “that walked to Albany and back”. Indoors and at events he wears his good shirt. His prop is an old palo drum, held between his knees or on his shoulder, and a chewed yellow pencil stub.
•	Variant [RAFA-1991]: About 30, hair cut short, playing the palo on the stone ring.

Key props (character-linked)
•	The coin. See the prop sheet. JC keeps it in his right front jeans pocket and it runs warm.
•	La Madre (Atabey). See the prop sheet.
•	Yúcahu. See the prop sheet.
•	Chismosa. JC’s recorder: the contact-mic module. See the prop sheet.
•	Vale’s black notebook. A small black notebook, pocket-sized, with an elastic band that hangs loose. It lives in his breast pocket and he writes in a small, neat hand. Anthony and Alan carry plain earlier pocket notebooks. At 10:07 in Ch. 23 its pages fade pale “like a receipt left in the sun”.
•	Manny’s conga. A worn conga with a natural skin head and dark wood staves (adaptation choice: dark mahogany). A crack already runs in the rim when it enters the Armory. In Ch. 21 the rim cracks end to end when it hits the carpet. In Ch. 24 it arrives wrapped in a pillowcase wearing a CONGA visitor sticker with a smiley face. After the Hunts Point repair it shows a visible repair band around the rim, still there in 2057. The café’s separate old loose-headed conga, tilted with “the personality of my uncle”, is used only in Ch. 4.
•	The Pyrex. A clear glass covered baking dish. In Ch. 8 it holds sixteen pounds of baked macaroni. In Ch. 24 it comes back washed, full of arroz con habichuelas, steaming under foil on the BENDICIONES mat. An earlier Pyrex of arroz con habichuelas sits on JC’s desk in Ch. 1.
•	Mamá’s glasses. A set of eight Pathmark drinking glasses. One shatters in Ch. 15, leaving seven. The “stranger” eighth, bought in Ch. 18, has a thick base and an air bubble in its side.
•	The Café Bustelo can. A red can with a soft plastic lid, holding rubber-banded cash and a note in slanted handwriting. In Ch. 24 it is empty.
•	Aperture speaker. A small white speaker with a camera-iris decal (overlapping blades). Variations: on a bracket over a colmado, between chandelier hooks, or wrapped in a dish towel tied with kitchen string on top of a fridge. In Ch. 24 it hangs on a nail with “RIP” written in Sharpie on a paper plate.
•	Calm Block app. A soft, rounded gentle-green leaf icon. At 9:40 the screens show a single bar of rain.
